    How this generator works

    The myNoise idea, rebuilt with live synthesis — nothing here is a recording on a loop.

    Ten channels, one scene

    • Every soundscape is ten separate sound sources playing at once — rain bands, surf layers, fire crackle, crickets, and more.
    • Each fader is one source. Raise only the ones you like; silence the rest. There is no single “right” mix.
    • Event channels (thunder, birds, crackles, droplets) fire at random intervals, so the scene never repeats exactly.

    Ways to listen

    1. Start from a preset, then nudge two or three faders — small changes shift the whole mood.
    2. Animate lets the faders drift slowly on their own, the way real weather changes over an hour.
    3. Pure Noise is a spectrum mixer: its faders are frequency bands from 32 Hz to 16 kHz. Pink and Brown presets are the classic sleep and focus shapes.
    4. Tap a channel name to mute just that channel; tap again to bring it back. Double-click a name to solo it — hear exactly what that layer contributes.
    5. Stack a second generator underneath the first, and calibrate the EQ once for your speakers and ears — both live below the mixer.

    For sleep & focus

    • Low, steady mixes (Distant Rain, Deep Swell, Brown noise) mask household noise without demanding attention.
    • Set the sleep timer and let the fade-out carry you off — no sudden silence to wake you.
    • Keep master volume modest and let the low faders do the masking; very bright channels (Spray, Sparks, high bands) are best kept low at night.

    Stacks worth trying

    • Rainy Day Café + Rain on Leaves underneath at ~40% — the café sits indoors, the weather stays outside.
    • Blizzard Cabin + Crackling Fire — the stove channels double up and the storm stays at the walls.
    • Summer Night + Thunderstorm at low level — crickets in front, a storm working miles behind them.
    • Train Journey + Pure Noise (Brown preset) — rail rhythm over a deep masking bed for noisy rooms.

    Animate, three ways

    • Drift — every fader random-walks on its own clock; the classic myNoise slider animation.
    • Wave — the faders rise and fall in sequence, left to right, like weather moving through the scene.
    • Breathe — the whole mix swells and settles together around your settings, on a slow cycle.
    • Wave and Breathe move around the mix you set — your balance stays the center of the motion.

    Tones & the bell

    • Drone & Tones tunes a harmonic stack like an instrument; its binaural pair (200 Hz left, 210 Hz right) produces a slow 10 Hz beat in headphones.
    • The meditation bell rings once when you turn it on, then every five minutes over whatever is playing.
    • Combine the bell with the sleep timer: bells mark the time while you're awake, and the fade-out still ends the session.
